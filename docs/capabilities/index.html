01 — Milling

5-Axis CNC Milling

Eight five-axis machining centres for geometries that would need five setups on 3-axis machines — or cannot be made on them at all. Impellers, turbine components, complex housings, orthopaedic trial parts.

Single-setup machining removes cumulative tolerance stack-up: position accuracy between features stays within ±0.01 mm regardless of part complexity. Tilting the part instead of extending the tool also lets short, rigid cutters reach deep pockets — better finish, no chatter, faster feeds.

Not every part needs five axes. A 100-piece order of flat brackets on a 3-axis machine can cost 40% less than the same parts on a 5-axis machine, at identical quality. We route jobs by what the part needs, not by which machine looks impressive in a quotation.

02 — Turning

CNC Turning & Mill-Turn

Forty-plus lathes including live-tooling mill-turn centres, covering a Ø1 mm to Ø300 mm envelope.

The interesting parts are the ones that are round and then not-quite-round: cross-holes, flats, milled slots and axial ports on turned blanks. Doing those in one setup on a mill-turn centre removes the second fixture — and with it, the concentricity error that comes from re-chucking.

  • In-process probing verifies critical diameters without unclamping
  • Bar feed to Ø80 mm for unattended production runs
  • Thread whirling for long, thin, high-precision threads
  • Sub-spindle pick-off completes the back of the part in cycle
03 — Precision

Tolerances & Precision

Standard tolerance ISO 2768-m; capability to ±0.005 mm on critical dimensions, ±0.002 mm with grinding support. CMM verification on first articles; SPC charts for production runs above 500 pieces.

BandWhat it means in practice
±0.1 mm
ISO 2768-m		 The default for any competent shop. Correct for housings, brackets and non-mating features on an unmarked drawing.
±0.05 mm Comfortable precision work with good process control. Most “precision parts” live here without drama.
±0.01 mm Precision territory. Requires controlled processes, temperature-stable inspection, and often single-setup machining. Bearing bores and precision locating features.
±0.005 mm Capability ceiling. CMM verification mandatory, sometimes grinding as a secondary process. Gauge elements, high-pressure sealing fits, optical mounts.

Mark what matters. Quoting every feature at ±0.005 mm prices the whole part like a gauge block. Mark the functional dimensions on the drawing and let the rest ride at standard tolerance — that habit typically saves 15–25% on precision parts.

04 — Finishing

Finishing & Surface Treatment

Finishing is coordinated and inspected by us, with samples approved before the batch runs. Colour consistency is managed per order — anodise batches are tracked so batch 200 matches batch 2.

  • Anodising — Type II decorative and Type III hard anodise, chromic and sulphuric, with colour matching across a batch
  • Passivation — per ASTM A967, standard on stainless medical and marine parts
  • Black oxide — tooling, fixtures and wear surfaces
  • Electroplating — zinc, nickel, electroless nickel
  • Bead blasting — uniform matte prep before anodise, and a finished texture in its own right
  • Laser engraving — part marking, serialisation and UID compliance
