How to Prepare CAD Files for a CNC Machining RFQ (Get Better Quotes Faster)

The quality of your RFQ determines the quality of your quote. Two customers send identical parts; one gets a precise 24-hour quotation with price breaks, the other gets three days of email ping-pong asking for files that open, tolerances that matter, and quantities that are real. Here's what separates the two — from the shop side of the inbox.

File formats: what works and what doesn't

Send native-neutral 3D formats: STEP (.stp/.step) or IGES (.igs). STEP is our preference — solid geometry, no surface stitching surprises. Avoid:

  • Native CAD files (SolidWorks, Fusion 360) — version mismatches cause import errors; export STEP instead
  • STL alone — mesh files need remodeling; fine for rough shape reference, useless for quoting exact geometry
  • 2D-only — DXF/PDF drawings quote as "best guess" unless dimensions fully define 3D geometry, which they almost never do

The winning combo: STEP file + 2D PDF drawing where the PDF marks critical tolerances, finishes and threads the CAD doesn't capture.

Tolerances: mark what matters, skip what doesn't

Quoting every feature at ±0.005mm when the part needs it on two bores prices the whole part like a gauge block. Conversely, an unmarked drawing defaults to ISO 2768-m in our quotation — fine for most features, wrong for the press-fit bore. Mark the critical dimensions on the PDF (the ones that affect function), let the rest ride at standard tolerance. This one habit typically saves 15–25% on precision parts because we machine to the process the part needs, not the paranoia level of a blanket spec.

Threads: the silent quote-killer

Model threads cosmetically (or note them) and specify on the drawing: size, pitch, depth and class. "Add threads where needed" has produced some creative interpretations in this industry. Standard callouts — M6×1.0 depth 12, 1/4-20 UNC — quote instantly; ambiguities become emails.

Quantities: tell us the real number

"1 piece for now, maybe 50 later" quotes differently than "5 now, 50 in Q3." We structure pricing with price breaks and note the fixture/process decisions that make the transition cheap. Fixtures designed on prototype day make production day inexpensive — but only if we know production is coming.

Finishes and materials: state them or invite assumptions

Alloy, temper, anodize type/color, passivation, surface roughness targets. "Aluminum, make it look nice" gets our default (6061-T6, clear anodize) — maybe exactly right, maybe not what the design intended. One sentence prevents one re-make.

The checklist before you hit send

  1. STEP file — solids, watertight
  2. 2D PDF — critical tolerances marked, threads specified
  3. Material + finish stated
  4. Real quantities (current + expected)
  5. Target delivery date

That's it — 90% of quote friction disappears with those five lines. Upload through our RFQ form and the answer lands within 24 hours, engineers included in the reply.
