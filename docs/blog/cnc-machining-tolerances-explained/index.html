Understanding CNC Machining Tolerances: From ±0.1mm to ±0.005mm

Tolerance callouts are where design intent and machining cost collide — usually because "as tight as possible" appears on drawings that don't need it. Understanding what tolerance grades actually cost (and why) lets you tighten only the features that matter and keeps quotes sane. Here's the map we wish every customer had.

What standard CNC machining delivers

  • General features, ISO 2768-m (≈±0.1mm on a 100mm dimension): the default for any competent shop. If your drawing is unmarked, this is what you'll get — and for housings, brackets and non-mating features, it's correct.
  • ±0.05mm: comfortable standard work with good process control. Most "precision parts" live here without drama.
  • ±0.01mm: precision territory. Requires controlled processes, temperature-stable inspection, and often single-setup machining. Bores with bearings, precision locating features.
  • ±0.005mm and below: the shop's capability ceiling territory. CMM verification mandatory, sometimes grinding as secondary process. Genuinely needed for gauge elements, high-pressure sealing fits, optical mounts.

Why tighter costs more — the honest breakdown

Cost doesn't scale linearly; it scales with risk. At ±0.1mm, first parts pass; scrap is near zero. At ±0.01mm, every part gets CMM-checked, tool wear windows shrink, temperature matters (a 10°C shop drift moves a 100mm steel feature by 0.012mm — physics, not skill). At ±0.005mm, we're measuring with uncertainty close to the tolerance itself. A part that's ±0.05mm everywhere versus ±0.05mm general with ±0.005mm on two bores can differ 2–3x in price. Same part, same shop — different process discipline.

Geometric tolerances people forget

Linear ± tolerances get all the attention, but GD&T callouts frequently cost more: flatness on a sealing face, true position on a bore pattern, perpendicularity on a datum. A ±0.05mm linear tolerance with a 0.01mm flatness callout is a flatness job, not a size job. When engineers review RFQs, geometric callouts drive process selection more than dimension boxes do — mark your datums properly and the quote reflects reality.

Practical rules from our quoting desk

  1. Tighten only functional features: mating bores, sealing faces, alignment features. Everything else rides standard.
  2. Watch the smallest feature: a Ø0.3mm hole at ±0.005mm is a different process than a Ø10mm bore at the same number. Feature size changes capability math.
  3. Ask for capability, not promises: request the CMM report with first articles — numbers on a report beat numbers in an email, every time.
  4. Prototype at production tolerance: if production needs ±0.01mm, prototypes at ±0.1mm prove nothing about manufacturability. We machine prototypes at the production spec so your design validation is honest.

The 24-hour reality check

Send the drawing with tolerances marked — critical features flagged, general features standard. The quotation returns with our capability assessment per feature, honest pricing on the tight ones, and a note where a tolerance looks like a copy-paste artifact rather than design intent. We've saved customers money by pointing out their own drawings before; it's included in the quote, not an upcharge.
